§ 01 · WHO

One person builds it. One person answers for it.

Bailey Booth. I write and I build. Every word of doctrine on this site, every drawing on it, the intake that carried your enquiry to my inbox, and the machinery behind it. One pair of hands on the judgment. The repeating work is carried by the same cells this company installs.

There is no account manager. There is no delivery team standing behind a founder's name. The person who audits your business writes the brief, builds the cell, releases it, and picks up the phone when it fails on a Sunday.

Accountability is not delegated here. That is a law before it is a headcount: judgment stays human, and the consequence never leaves this company. A firm built this way cannot break that rule by accident. The constraint that comes with it is capacity, and capacity is managed by taking fewer installations, not by putting a stranger between you and the work.

§ 02 · THE ARGUMENT CAME FIRST

The diagnosis came first. This company is the construction.

I did not arrive at automation through software. I arrived at it through an argument about what this process is doing to the people inside it.

Psychoanthrofuturism: AI and Cortical Accelerationism was published before any of this was for sale. Its thesis is exact, and the book is disciplined about what carries it. The mechanism is documented: systems that optimise against measured attention, running a feedback loop closed on each person at latencies no earlier medium came near. The cortical claim is inference drawn from that mechanism, that attention, reward, and the cadence of a person's own interior speech are retrained in those most heavily exposed. The empirical record behind that inference is young and contested, the book says so, and it prints the evidence that would defeat it. State the claim, grade what supports it, name what would break it. That method is the reason this company exists in the form it does.

From there the book refuses the two easy positions. Resistance in the old sense is compromised, because the faculty that would conduct it is implicated in the operation it would resist. The book concedes that this argument, held without limit, would prove nothing and disarm its own author, so it restricts the seal to what a person can report about himself and stakes the thesis where measurement can reach it. Nostalgia is a delay wearing the costume of a virtue. What is left is to proceed deliberately instead of drifting.

Two findings from that argument are the reason this company exists rather than some other one.

The first is that roles fail under symbolic velocity, the speed at which signifiers can be reproduced once the structures that anchored them are gone. A role was never only a function. It was a function held in place by a structure of obligation, and when that structure thins the title keeps circulating with less and less behind it. A business is a lattice of roles. As they thin, what is actually left, and the only thing that can honestly be installed, is the repeating work itself. That is why the unit here is the workflow and not the company, and why the size of the business was never the unit.

The second concerns responsibility rather than machinery. The danger is not that machines act. It is that judgment is transferred: consult the model instead of the person and the risk of being wrong is removed, and the capacity goes with it. So this company delegates execution and refuses, in writing, to delegate judgment. Nothing verifies its own work. Every action is recorded. Every cell ends at a named human being. That is not caution bolted onto a product. It is the diagnosis honoured.

Among the conditions the book sets out is the construction of new institutional forms, built outside the institutions, by whoever still has the attention left to build them. DAELIX is one attempt at that, conducted in the open where it can be checked.

The book gave the process a diagnosis. This company gives it a body: boundary, limit, a record, and a place where it stops.

Psychoanthrofuturism: AI and Cortical Accelerationism is the book described here, and it sits alongside The Social Theatre, The Diagnostics and The Imitation Principle. None of the four is offered as evidence that this company works. The first is the reason the company exists.

§ 03 · PRIOR OPERATING EXPERIENCE

I ran it before I sold it.

Doctrine is cheap. Anyone can publish a drawing of a machine they have never run.

In a separate organisation, multiple agents have been operating unattended since May. Editorial and engineering design workflows are in action and running, each on its own schedule. Every output is checked against a schema before it moves. A guard halts the chain in silence rather than let something wrong reach an audience. Full cycles have completed end to end with no hand on them.

It has also failed, and the failures are the part worth reading. A parser demanded a field the model was right to omit, and the chain died rather than publish something malformed. A model intermittently wrapped its output in a shape the contract did not name. Both were found in execution logs rather than by someone complaining, and both were fixed at the contract level rather than by retrying until it worked.

That is not a DAELIX client installation and it is not offered as one. It is a separate organisation and no client work has been done. What it proves is narrower than a case study and worth more. The architecture is not something I have read about. I run it daily, across many workflows, and I have watched it break in specific ways and corrected the standard rather than the symptom each time. The same anatomy carries this company: the intake that brought your enquiry, the record behind it, and the checks that stopped it reaching you malformed. Nothing here is a machine I keep at arm's length from my own operation.

What it does not prove: that the same anatomy survives a legacy system, thirty years of inconsistent data, or a service level somebody else's revenue depends on. That gets proven inside an installation or it does not get proven, and I will not claim it before then.

§ 04 · WHERE THIS COMPANY STANDS

The first installations are being taken now.

What is published instead is the standard: the anatomy, the six laws, the governance page with its stated limits, and the exact shape of every artefact you would receive, with the fields left blank because your numbers are the only ones that will ever fill them.

The audit is a paid, fixed-fee engagement at £750, credited in full against an installation if you proceed within thirty days. If it finds nothing worth automating it says so in writing, and that is the end of it.

The first installations are priced as first installations, in writing, with the reason stated: you are carrying the risk of being early, and the price reflects it. What you get for it is the founder on the work and a record of every action taken inside your business.

What is being built here is not a practice with one operator in it. It is a standard: an anatomy, a law, and a record, written so that it can be run by people who did not write it. The first installations are mine because they have to be. The point of publishing the standard is that eventually they will not be.
